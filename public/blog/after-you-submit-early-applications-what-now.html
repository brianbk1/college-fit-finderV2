You Submitted Your Early Applications. Now What?

You hit submit before the November 1 deadline. Take a breath—that was the hard part. But the weeks between now and mid-December aren’t a dead zone, and a few small things you do now can save a lot of stress later. Here’s a short, practical list.

1. Verify, don’t assume

Submitting an application is not the same as a college receiving everything it needs. Within a few days of applying, most schools email you a link to an applicant portal. Log in to each one and confirm that your application, your counselor and teacher recommendations, your transcript, and any test scores are all marked received. If something shows as missing, you have time to fix it now—which you won’t in late December.

Check the portal, not just your inbox. Missing items usually appear in the college’s applicant portal, not in an email. A quiet inbox doesn’t mean everything arrived.

2. Finish the financial aid side

Admission and affordability are two separate processes, and the money side has its own deadlines. Make sure you’ve submitted the FAFSA, and check whether any of your schools also require the CSS Profile for their own aid. Deadlines vary by college and can be earlier for early applicants, so confirm each school’s aid deadlines directly. Getting this done now keeps a future acceptance from coming with a financial surprise.

3. Keep your grades up—this really matters

Your senior-year performance isn’t just a formality. Colleges receive a mid-year report with your first-semester senior grades, and offers of admission are made on the expectation that you’ll keep performing. A serious slide can genuinely put an offer at risk. The fall you’re in right now is still part of your application.

4. Build your Regular Decision list anyway

This is the step students most want to skip—and the one that pays off most. Early results, whether admit, deny, or defer, are far easier to handle when your next round of applications is already in motion. Keep researching schools, keep drafting those essays, and have your Regular Decision list ready to go before winter break. If you applied Early Decision somewhere binding, you’ll withdraw the others if you’re admitted—but having them ready costs you nothing and protects you if the news isn’t what you hoped.

5. Understand what a “defer” would mean

If an early application is deferred, it’s not a rejection—your application simply moves into the Regular Decision pool for another look. Some colleges welcome a brief update or a letter of continued interest; others don’t want extra materials. If it happens, follow each school’s stated instructions rather than guessing. Knowing this now makes a December email far less alarming.

When to expect news

Most early decisions arrive in mid-to-late December. Until then, the best thing you can do is the quiet, unglamorous work: confirm everything landed, finish the aid forms, protect your grades, and keep your options open. You’ve done the big lift—now just don’t coast.
